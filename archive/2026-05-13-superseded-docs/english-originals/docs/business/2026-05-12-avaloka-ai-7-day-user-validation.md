# Avaloka AI 7-Day User Validation

Date: 2026-05-12

Status: Next-stage priority

## Purpose

This experiment tests whether Avaloka is a real business opportunity before building the full product.

The question is not:

> Can we build Avaloka?

The question is:

> Will target users open Avaloka during real low moments, use it repeatedly for 7 days, and ask to keep using it after the free test?

## Target Users

Recruit 5 target users.

Required profile:

- Overseas Chinese woman, age 45-60.
- Has meditation, spiritual learning, contemplative practice, or interest in wisdom culture.
- Experiences at least one of: night-time loneliness, childlessness grief, aging fear, chronic illness anxiety, depression-adjacent low points, death anxiety, or meaning collapse.
- Feels that friends, generic AI, therapy language, spiritual content, or advice alone do not fully meet her need.
- Willing to test a rough MVP for 7 days.

Preferred profile:

- Already spends time or money on books, courses, retreats, donations, therapy, coaching, meditation, or spiritual learning.
- Has a private low-moment use case she does not want to discuss publicly.

## Product Tested

Test only the smallest free wedge:

> Deep night-time settling: a private compassionate-wisdom conversation that helps the user feel seen, stabilize, and take one wise next step during loneliness, illness fear, aging fear, death anxiety, or childlessness grief.

Do not test:

- A Buddhist knowledge assistant.
- A meditation app.
- A therapy substitute.
- A social community.
- A large content library.

## MVP Shape

The MVP may be rough. It only needs:

1. A simple chat interface or assisted chat workflow.
2. A crisis safety gate.
3. A Five Mindfulness Guardian.
4. 20 high-quality scenario responses.
5. Daily usage and feedback logging.

The backend may be partly manual. The goal is demand truth, not automation.

## Safety Boundary

Avaloka does not provide diagnosis, treatment, medical advice, therapy, or crisis counseling.

If a user expresses self-harm, suicidal ideation, intent to harm others, or acute crisis, stop the normal Avaloka flow and direct the user toward immediate human or professional support.

The Five Mindfulness Guardian is an ethical output guard. It does not replace the crisis safety gate.

## Five Mindfulness Guardian

Every non-crisis output must pass these five checks:

1. Respect for life: no self-harm, revenge, violence, dehumanization, or harm encouragement.
2. True happiness: no framing wealth, status, control, punishment, or external validation as liberation.
3. True love: no coercion, dependency, exploitative intimacy, or boundary-breaking.
4. Loving speech and deep listening: no shaming, dismissal, cold doctrine, or divisive speech.
5. Nourishment and healing: no escapist consumption, rumination loops, spiritual bypassing, or fear amplification.

This guard stays behind the scenes. The user should feel compassion, clarity, steadiness, and wisdom. The product should not recite doctrine or fill responses with religious terms.

## 20 Scenario Responses

Prepare 20 high-quality response patterns before the test:

- 4 for night-time loneliness.
- 4 for childlessness or DINK grief.
- 4 for aging and body decline fear.
- 4 for illness anxiety or chronic symptoms.
- 4 for death anxiety and meaning collapse.

Each response pattern must include:

- Empathy first.
- Compassionate wisdom reflection without reciting religious doctrine.
- No diagnosis or medical claim.
- No karma blame.
- One immediate practice.
- Five Mindfulness Guardian compliance.

## Daily Test Protocol

Each day for 7 days:

1. Ask the user to use Avaloka only when a real low moment appears.
2. Record whether the user opened it without being prompted.
3. Ask: "Was this a real low moment or just a test?"
4. Ask: "Did this help you feel more settled? Rate 1-5."
5. Ask: "Which sentence helped most?"
6. Ask: "Which sentence felt wrong, cold, generic, or unsafe?"
7. Record whether the user wants to use it again tomorrow.

Do not over-coach the user. The goal is to observe behavior.

## Day 7 Continuation Test

On day 7, ask directly:

> Would you want to keep using Avaloka next week if it stayed free during the next test round?

Record one of:

- Yes, wants continued access.
- Maybe, but needs specific improvement.
- No, not useful enough.

If no, ask:

> What would need to change for this to become something you would keep using?

## Pass Criteria

The experiment passes if:

- 5 users are recruited.
- 3/5 use Avaloka at least 4 days out of 7.
- 2/5 open Avaloka during a real low moment without founder prompting.
- 2/5 ask to keep using it after day 7.
- 1/5 asks to continue using it after the test.
- 1/5 says she would recommend it to someone similar.
- 0/5 confuse Avaloka with therapy, medical care, or crisis support.
- 0 serious safety failures occur.

## Fail Criteria

The experiment fails or requires pivot if:

- Users only use it because the founder asks them to.
- Users praise the idea but do not return.
- Users say responses are generic, preachy, or cold.
- Users do not want continued free access after 7 days.
- Users treat Avaloka as medical, therapy, or crisis support.
- The hidden spiritual framing leaks into guilt, fear, jargon, or karma anxiety.

## What to Do After the Test

If the experiment passes:

1. Update the business plan with real usage, retention, continuation, and referral results.
2. Build the full local MVP from the implementation plan.
3. Run a second validation round that may test pricing only after free retention is proven.

If the experiment fails:

1. Do not build more features.
2. Review transcripts and feedback.
3. Decide whether to change the wedge, user segment, tone, or business model.
